Phase 2 · Lesson 8

Duty Cycle Adjuster (DCA / DCM)

Another "make high speed work" feature: keep the clock's high and low halves equal so timing margin isn't wasted.

The problem: a lopsided clock

A perfect clock spends 50% of each cycle high and 50% low. At high speed the clock can drift to, say, 55/45 - duty-cycle distortion. Since DDR transfers data on both edges (Phase 0, Lesson 4), an uneven clock makes one half-cycle shorter than the other, eating into the already-tiny timing margin.

What DCA/DCM does

DDR5 adds a Duty Cycle Monitor (DCM) to measure the distortion and a Duty Cycle Adjuster (DCA) to correct it, nudging the clock back toward an even 50/50 so both the rising-edge and falling-edge data beats get equal timing margin.

Pattern recognition DFE (Lesson 7) and DCA are both high-speed enablers - extra circuitry whose only job is to make DDR5's aggressive data rates survivable. DDR4 didn't need them at its lower speeds.
