Phase 2 · Lesson 4

On-Die ECC (ODECC)

New in DDR5: every chip now quietly corrects its own single-bit errors - separate from the system ECC you met in Phase 1.

Error correction, inside the chip

DDR4: error correction was a system/module feature - an extra chip on the DIMM, managed by the controller.
DDR5: every chip now has its own internal ECC that corrects single-bit errors inside the chip before data ever leaves it.

Why DDR5 needs it

DDR5 cells sit on smaller, denser process nodes, so raw single-bit errors are simply more common. On-die ECC cleans them at the source. It is present even on a "non-ECC" consumer module - it is about yield and reliability of the chip itself, not the system-level ECC.

The read-modify-write catch Because the chip stores ECC bits alongside each data block, a partial write (smaller than the protected block) forces an internal read-modify-write: read the block, merge the new bytes, recompute ECC, write it back. Worth knowing when reasoning about write performance.
