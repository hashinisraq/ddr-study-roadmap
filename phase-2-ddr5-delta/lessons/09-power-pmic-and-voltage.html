Power on the DIMM: PMIC & Lower Voltage

DDR5 moves power regulation onto the module itself and drops the voltage - both help the high-speed signaling stay clean.

Regulation moves onto the module

DDR4: the motherboard supplied the DRAM voltage rails.
DDR5: a Power Management IC (PMIC) sits on the DIMM and regulates power right where it is consumed.

Lower voltage, plus sensors

  • Core voltage drops from 1.2 V (DDR4) → 1.1 V (DDR5) - less energy for the same work.
  • DDR5 modules also carry an SPD hub (module identity/config) and on-module temperature sensors.
Why on-DIMM power matters At gigahertz speeds, clean, stable voltage close to the chips directly affects signal integrity. Putting regulation on the module (and lowering the voltage) improves power quality and shifts the burden from the motherboard to the DIMM - one reason DDR5 can scale to very high data rates.
