Two Independent Sub-channels

The biggest architectural change from DDR4 - and the one every other DDR5 feature is built around.

One wide channel → two narrow ones

DDR4: a DIMM presents one 64-bit channel.
DDR5: the same DIMM is split into two independent 32-bit sub-channels (A and B), each with its own command/address bus.

Why split it

Two narrow channels that work on different requests simultaneously keep the memory busier than one wide channel that can only do one thing at a time. Modern many-core CPUs generate lots of small, scattered accesses - exactly the workload that benefits from more independent channels.

This drives the next lesson Halving the channel width to 32 bits is why DDR5 doubles the burst length to 16 (next lesson) - together they still move one 64-byte cache line per access while gaining the two-independent-channels advantage. With ECC, each sub-channel is 40 bits (32 data + 8 ECC).
Check yourself
  1. How many channels does a DDR5 DIMM present, and how wide is each?
  2. What does "independent" buy you over one wide channel?
