The Speed Roadmap

Where all the previous DDR5 features were heading: much higher data rates than DDR4 could reach.

DDR4 vs DDR5 data rates

DDR4 topped out around 3200 MT/s (standard JEDEC). DDR5 starts at 4800 MT/s and climbs well beyond, with the roadmap reaching toward 8800 MT/s.

Putting Phase 2 together Every DDR5 change serves this curve. Two sub-channels + more banks feed the bandwidth; BL16 keeps the cache-line granularity; on-die ECC + RFM keep dense, fast chips reliable; DFE + DCA keep the signal readable; PMIC + 1.1 V keep the power clean. That is the whole story of DDR5 as an upgrade over DDR4.
