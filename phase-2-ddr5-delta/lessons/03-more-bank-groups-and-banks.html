Phase 2 · Lesson 3

More Bank Groups & Banks

A quieter change than sub-channels, but it directly feeds performance: DDR5 simply has more independent banks to interleave across.

More of a good thing

Recall from Phase 1 that bank-level parallelism and bank groups are how DRAM hides its slow row operations, and that interleaving across different bank groups lets the controller issue accesses back-to-back at the fast tCCD_S rate.

DDR5 increases both counts - commonly up to 8 bank groups and 32 banks per device in larger configurations (versus DDR4's typical 4 groups / 16 banks). More banks = more rows open at once = more opportunities to overlap work and keep the data bus busy.

Bank groups (typical)Banks (typical)
DDR4up to 4up to 16
DDR5up to 8up to 32
Why it matters More bank groups give the controller more ways to spread consecutive accesses so they land in different groups and issue at the fast rate. Combined with the two sub-channels, DDR5 has far more independent access points than DDR4 - essential for feeding modern core counts.
