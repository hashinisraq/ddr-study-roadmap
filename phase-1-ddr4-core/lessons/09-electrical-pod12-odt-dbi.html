Electrical Essentials: POD12, ODT, DBI

Just enough electrical reality to understand how DDR4 drives its signals cleanly and cheaply.

POD12 signaling at 1.2 V

DDR4 uses Pseudo-Open-Drain (POD12) I/O at VDDQ = 1.2 V (DDR3 was 1.5 V). Lower voltage means lower power. POD terminates to the supply rail, which pairs naturally with the on-die termination below.

On-Die Termination (ODT)

From Phase 0, Lesson 7: high-speed traces echo, and a matched resistor kills the echo. DDR4 puts that resistor inside the chip - ODT - and switches it on only for the chip currently being addressed, so reflections are absorbed exactly where needed. Dynamic ODT can change the termination value on the fly during writes.

Data Bus Inversion (DBI)

With POD signaling, driving many 0s costs more power and adds noise. If a data byte would put too many 0s on the bus, the chip inverts the whole byte and flags it on the DBI pin. The receiver un-inverts it. Fewer 0s on the wires → less power and cleaner signals.

Tie-back These three are the practical answers to Phase 0's signal-integrity chain: lower voltage for power, termination for reflections, and DBI to tame the POD bus. You set ODT and DBI via the mode registers (Lesson 8).
