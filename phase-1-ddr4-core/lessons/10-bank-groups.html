Bank Groups - DDR4's Signature Trick

The feature that lets DDR4 sustain higher throughput than DDR3 at the same core speed.

Banks grouped into groups

DDR4 organizes its banks into bank groups - e.g. 4 groups of 4 banks = 16 banks. The clever part is the timing difference between accessing the same group versus a different one.

Why the controller loves this

If the controller spreads consecutive accesses across different bank groups, it issues them back-to-back at the fast tCCD_S rate and sustains higher bandwidth. Two accesses to the same group must wait the longer tCCD_L.

The takeaway Bank groups add a cheap form of parallelism: interleave across groups and the data bus stays busy. This is the main reason DDR4 beats DDR3's effective bandwidth at equal core speed - and DDR5 doubles down with even more bank groups (Phase 2).
