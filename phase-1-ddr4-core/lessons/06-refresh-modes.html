Phase 1 · Lesson 6

Refresh Modes

Phase 0 said cells must be refreshed every ~64 ms. Here is how DDR4 actually does it, and the trade-offs between the modes.

Auto-refresh (normal operation)

During normal use the controller issues REFRESH (REF) commands at the tREFI pace (about one every 7.8 µs). The chip tracks internally which rows to refresh next - the controller just has to send REF often enough. Each REF occupies the chip for tRFC.

Self-refresh (low-power sleep)

When the system idles, the chip enters self-refresh: it refreshes itself using its own internal timer, so the controller - and even the external clock - can shut down. This is what lets a sleeping laptop keep RAM contents at minimal power.

Temperature-controlled refresh

Hot DRAM leaks faster. With temperature-controlled refresh the chip adjusts its refresh rate to temperature - refreshing more often when hot, less when cool, saving power.

Fine Granularity Refresh (1× / 2× / 4×)

Instead of one big refresh, DDR4 can split it into smaller, more frequent chunks (2× or 4× the rate, each with a shorter tRFC). Smaller chunks block the chip for less time at once - trading a bit more total overhead for lower worst-case latency.

The trade-off in one line Refresh is pure overhead - time the chip can't serve requests. Every mode is about covering all cells in time while stealing as little performance/power as possible. DDR5 pushes this further with same-bank refresh (Phase 2).
