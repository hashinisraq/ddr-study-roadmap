A Reading Order That Works

How to actually get value from the spec without drowning: four steps that connect what you already know to the exact text and numbers.

The four steps

  1. Trace your model on their state diagram. Take the ACT→RD→PRE walk from Phase 1 and follow it on the spec's own simplified state diagram. Confirm your mental model matches theirs.
  2. Read one command at a time, with its timing diagram. In the command/ operation section, read a single command and study its timing diagram alongside - the diagrams are the spec's best teaching tool, so follow the arrows.
  3. Jump to the AC timing table and look up the numbers for the command you just read. Connect "the rule" (operation section) to "the number" (timing table).
  4. Only then dip into the electrical and IDD sections, and only as deep as your track needs.
Why this order You already built the concepts in Phases 0-2. This order attaches those concepts to the spec's exact wording, diagrams, and numbers - turning understanding into the ability to cite chapter and verse. Reading linearly from page 1 would bury you in pinouts and electrical tables before you reach the parts that teach.
Analogy You don't learn a city by reading the phone book front to back. You take a route you already know and look up the exact street names and numbers as you go. Same here: known route (ACT→RD→PRE) first, then the precise spec details.
Check yourself
  1. What's the first thing to do when you open the spec?
  2. Why read a command and its timing diagram together?
  3. When should you read the electrical/IDD sections?
