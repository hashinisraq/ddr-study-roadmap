Phase 3 · Lesson 3

Datasheets vs the JEDEC Spec

Two kinds of primary source, used together: the spec gives the rules, a datasheet gives a concrete real part.

What each one is

JEDEC spec (JESD79-x)Vendor datasheet
ScopeWhat any compliant DDR4/DDR5 chip must doOne real part from one vendor
ContentThe full standard: state machine, all features, all timing optionsThat part's density, organization, and chosen timings
Use it forThe rules and the complete pictureA concrete instance to make the rules real

Read them side by side

The spec can feel abstract; a datasheet is a specific chip you could actually buy. Open a production DDR4 datasheet and a DDR5 datasheet (Micron, Samsung, or SK Hynix - all free) next to the spec. When a spec concept feels vague, find it in the datasheet's tables and diagrams; when a datasheet number puzzles you, find the rule behind it in the spec.

The single best way to make the spec real Having both open at once is the highest-leverage habit in Phase 3. The spec tells you why a timing exists; the datasheet shows you its actual value for a real part at a real speed grade. Drop a couple of free datasheets into this phase's notes/ folder so they travel with your study.
Check yourself
  1. What does the JEDEC spec define that a datasheet doesn't?
  2. What does a datasheet add that the spec doesn't?
  3. Why read them together?
