Change a Timing and Predict the Result

The exercise that turns timing parameters from trivia into a model you can reason with - predict first, then verify.

The experiment

In your simulator (Lesson 3), change one timing at a time, rerun, and predict the effect before you look:

ChangePredict
Loosen tRCD (slower row open)Every row open costs more → random-heavy workloads slow down most (more opens).
Loosen tFAW (fewer activations per window)Bursty multi-bank workloads throttle - you can't open rows as fast.
Tighten CLLower read latency; helps latency-bound more than bandwidth-bound workloads.

Why prediction is the point

Anyone can change a number and read a result. The skill is predicting the result from the model and having it come true. When your prediction matches, the timings have stopped being arbitrary and become a working mental model.

This is the phase milestone Change tRCD and tFAW, correctly predict the effect on a streaming vs random workload, and explain it via row hits/misses and bank parallelism. If your explanation holds, you understand DDR at a working-engineer level.
Check yourself
  1. Which workload is hurt most by a looser tRCD, and why?
  2. What kind of workload does tFAW throttle?
  3. Did your predictions match the simulator? Where they didn't, what did you learn?
