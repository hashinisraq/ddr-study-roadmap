The Memory Stick (DIMM) and Its Chips

Let's look at the actual thing you hold in your hand and name its parts, in plain language.

The stick is called a DIMM

The memory "stick" you plug into a computer's motherboard is called a DIMM - short for Dual In-line Memory Module. "Module" just means a small board with parts on it. You don't need the long name; DIMM = one stick of RAM.

Laptops and small PCs use a shorter version called a SO-DIMM - same idea, smaller board.

The black rectangles are the memory chips

The small black rectangles soldered onto the board are the DRAM chips - the parts that actually store your data. A typical stick has eight of them (sometimes sixteen, or nine on special "ECC" sticks you'll meet later).

The gold strip along the bottom is a row of contacts (pins). When you push the stick into its slot, these connect the chips to the rest of the computer.

The plan from here We will keep zooming in: this lesson is the whole stick; the next looks at how the chips team up; then we go inside one chip (banks); and finally inside a bank to a single storage cell. Big picture first, details second.
Analogy The DIMM is a egg carton, and the chips are the eggs. The carton's job is to hold the eggs in a neat row and connect them to the kitchen (the motherboard).
