Phase 0 · Lesson 9

Digital Timing: Setup, Hold & Metastability

The rule that underlies every DDR timing number: a signal must be stable around the clock edge, or you get garbage.

Capturing a signal on a clock edge

Synchronous logic samples a signal at the instant of a clock edge. For that sample to be reliable, the data must already be steady for a short time before the edge and stay steady for a short time after it:

  • Setup time (tsu) - how long the data must be stable before the clock edge.
  • Hold time (th) - how long it must stay stable after the edge.
  • Clock-to-Q - after the edge, the small delay before the captured value appears at the output.

Break the rule → metastability

If the data changes right as the clock ticks (setup or hold violated), the capturing element can go metastable - hovering between 0 and 1 for a moment before settling randomly. Downstream logic may read it as either value: a real, intermittent bug.

Why this is the root of all DDR timings Numbers like tRCD, tRP, and CL in Phase 1 all exist to guarantee that whatever you are about to sample is stable in time when you grab it. Read every "wait N nanoseconds" as "wait until the signal is safely within its setup/hold window." At DDR speeds the windows are tiny - which is exactly why DDR needs careful training and equalization (later lessons).
Analogy Taking a sharp photo of a moving object: you need the subject to hold still just before and just after the shutter clicks. Snap while it is moving (violating setup/hold) and you get a blur - a value that is neither here nor there (metastable).
