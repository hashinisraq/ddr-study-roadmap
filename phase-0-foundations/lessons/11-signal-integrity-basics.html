Signal Integrity: Reflections & Termination (intuition)

Just the intuition you need now: at DDR speeds wires echo, echoes corrupt bits, and termination absorbs the echoes. The deep version waits for Phase 5.

At high speed, a wire is a transmission line

At low speed a trace is "just a wire" - change one end and the other follows. At DDR speeds the signal travels as a wave down the trace, and like any wave it can reflect off the far end if the end is not "matched." That reflection races back and smears into the next bit.

Termination fixes it

Placing a resistor matched to the line's impedance at the receiving end absorbs the wave's energy so it does not bounce. DDR puts this resistor inside the chip - On-Die Termination (ODT) - and switches it on only for the chip being talked to. (You will meet ODT as a real feature in Phase 1.)

How far to take this now For Phases 0-4 you only need the chain of ideas: high speed → the trace behaves like a transmission line → unmatched ends cause reflections → reflections corrupt bits → terminate to absorb them. The full treatment (impedance control, crosstalk, eye diagrams, equalization like DDR5's DFE) belongs to the signal-integrity track in Phase 5 - only go deep there if that is your direction.
Analogy Shout in an empty tiled room and the echo muddies your next words (reflection). Hang heavy curtains - matched absorption (termination) - and the echo disappears, so each word stays crisp.
