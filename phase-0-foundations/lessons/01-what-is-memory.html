What Is Memory (RAM)?

Start here with zero assumptions. Before any circuits or timings, let's be clear on what "memory" is and why a computer needs the fast kind.

Memory vs storage: your desk vs your cupboard

A computer keeps information in two very different places:

  • Storage (an SSD or hard drive) is like a big cupboard: it holds a lot, and it keeps everything even when the power is off - but it is slow to reach into.
  • Memory (RAM) is like your desk: small and temporary, but right in front of you and very fast to use. The computer puts whatever it is working on right now onto this "desk."

RAM stands for Random-Access Memory. It is the fast working space the computer uses while it runs programs.

Two key facts about RAM

  • It is temporary (volatile). When the power goes off, RAM forgets everything. That is why unsaved work is lost in a power cut - it was only on the "desk."
  • The CPU uses it constantly. Every program, every open browser tab, lives in RAM while running. So RAM's speed directly affects how fast your computer feels.
Where DDR fits in The RAM in modern computers is a specific kind called DDR SDRAM. "DDR4" and "DDR5" are just the 4th and 5th generations of it. This whole course is about understanding that memory - from this big picture all the way down to how a single bit is stored.
One-line analogy Storage is the filing cabinet (big, slow, permanent). RAM is the desk (small, fast, cleared each day). The CPU is you, working at the desk.
Check yourself
  1. What is the difference between RAM and storage?
  2. What happens to RAM's contents when the power is turned off?
  3. Why does RAM's speed matter for how fast a computer feels?
